## Man-Computer Symbiosis

### Links

* https://groups.csail.mit.edu/medg/people/psz/Licklider.html

### Notes

#### Summary

Licklider argues for a close partnership between humans and computers rather than either pure automation or humans using computers only as passive calculators. Humans should contribute goals, hypotheses, judgment, and evaluation; computers should handle fast, routine, data-heavy operations that prepare the ground for human insight.

#### Key ideas

* The goal is **cooperative intelligence**: people and computers should solve problems together.
* Computers should help with **formulating** problems, not merely execute solutions to problems that have already been fully specified.
* Humans are strong at setting goals, recognizing relevance, making judgments, and dealing with ambiguous situations.
* Computers are strong at repetitive calculation, searching, storage, bookkeeping, and rapidly manipulating formal representations.
* Tight interaction requires low-latency access. Licklider therefore emphasizes time-sharing and interactive computing rather than batch processing.
* Better memory organization, programming languages, displays, input devices, and communication between people and machines are prerequisites for this partnership.
* The paper anticipates many ideas later associated with interactive computing, personal computing, information retrieval, and human-AI collaboration.

#### Takeaway

The most useful computer is not necessarily one that replaces the human. It can be one that removes mechanical work and makes the human's reasoning loop faster, broader, and more precise.
