## Comparison of deep neural networks to spatio-temporal cortical dynamics of human visual object recognition reveals hierarchical correspondence

### Links

* https://www.nature.com/articles/srep27755

### Notes

#### Summary

The study compares representations inside a deep neural network trained for object recognition with the **spatial and temporal dynamics of human visual processing**.

#### Method

* Magnetoencephalography (MEG) measures when different visual representations emerge.
* Functional MRI (fMRI) measures where in the brain those representations are expressed.
* Representational similarity analysis compares patterns in the brain with patterns produced by different DNN layers.

#### Findings

* Early DNN layers correspond more strongly to early visual processing and posterior visual areas.
* Deeper layers correspond to representations appearing later in time and in higher-level regions along the ventral and dorsal visual streams.
* Network architecture contributes to brain-like representational structure, but architecture alone is not enough.
* Training on real-world object categorization is important for the full hierarchical correspondence to emerge.
* The result suggests that task demands can shape a hierarchy of representations in both artificial networks and biological visual systems.

#### Important nuance

The finding is a correspondence between representational structures, not evidence that the brain literally implements the same computations or architecture as the DNN.

#### Takeaway

A task-trained DNN can serve as a quantitative model for studying the progression of human object recognition. Comparing layer-by-layer representations with brain data provides a bridge between machine-learning models and systems neuroscience.
